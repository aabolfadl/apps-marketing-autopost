138 studies. 19,951 participants. One finding: monitoring your progress toward a goal makes you measurably more likely to reach it, and the effect is larger when the progress is physically recorded (Harkin et al., Psychological Bulletin, 2016).

Physically recorded. Not remembered, not estimated between sets. Written down, where you cannot quietly revise it afterwards.

Which matters, because roughly half of people who start a structured exercise programme have stopped within six months. Usually not from lack of effort. From losing the thread.

And if you are early in this: a new daily behaviour took a median of 66 days to become automatic, and in that same study missing one day did not break the process (Lally et al., 2010). Worth remembering on the day you miss one.
